# Fixed-Income Reference

Definitions, formulas, and worked examples for the core fixed-income
concepts used throughout the ALM toolkit.

Every number on this page is produced by
`scripts/fixed_income_examples.py`, which builds the same bonds and
swaps with `alm.asset.Bond`, `alm.core.InterestRateSwap`, `dv01`,
`dollar_convexity`, `immunize` and `irr`.  Run
`uv run python scripts/fixed_income_examples.py` to regenerate them.

---

## Duration (Macaulay)

**What it measures:**  The weighted-average time until a bond's cash
flows are received, where each weight is the present value of that
cash flow as a fraction of the bond's total price.

**Formula:**

$$
D = \frac{1}{P} \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{t}{f} \cdot \frac{CF_t}{(1 + y/f)^t}
$$

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| $P$ | Bond price (present value) |
| $CF_t$ | Cash flow at period $t$ |
| $y$ | Annual yield (nominal, compounded $f$ times a year) |
| $f$ | Payment frequency per year |
| $n$ | Total number of periods |

**Interpretation:**  A duration of 4.58 years means the bond's price
behaves like a zero-coupon bond maturing in 4.58 years.  Higher
duration = greater interest-rate sensitivity.

**Mini example — 5-year, 4 % semi-annual bond ($100 par, $y = 4\%$):**

| Period | Year | Cash Flow | PV @ 4 % | Weight | Year $\times$ Weight |
|--------|------|-----------|----------|--------|----------------------|
| 1 | 0.5 | $2.00 | $1.96 | 0.0196 | 0.010 |
| 2 | 1.0 | $2.00 | $1.92 | 0.0192 | 0.019 |
| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| 10 | 5.0 | $102.00 | $83.68 | 0.8368 | 4.184 |
| **Total** | | | **$100.00** | **1.0000** | **$D = 4.58$ yrs** |

*Generated by `scripts/fixed_income_examples.py`.*

**Code:** `Bond.duration()` in `src/alm/asset.py`

## Modified Duration

**What it measures:**  The percentage price change for a 1 % change in
yield.  Converts Macaulay duration into a direct risk measure.

**Formula:**

$$
D_{\text{mod}} = \frac{D}{1 + y/f}
$$

**Interpretation:**  For the bond above, $D_{\text{mod}} = 4.58 / 1.02
= 4.49$: a 1 % (100 bp) rise in yield causes approximately a 4.49 %
drop in price.

**Relationship to DV01:**

$$
D_{\text{mod}} = \frac{\text{DV01} \times 10{,}000}{P}
$$

The finite-difference DV01 of the same bond gives
$\text{DV01} \times 10{,}000 / P = 4.49$, matching the closed form.

*Generated by `scripts/fixed_income_examples.py`.*

## Convexity

**What it measures:**  The curvature of the price-yield relationship —
how duration itself changes as rates move.

**Formula:**

$$
C = \frac{1}{P \cdot f^2} \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{t(t+1) \cdot CF_t}{(1 + y/f)^{t+2}}
$$

**Interpretation:**  Convexity is always positive for option-free
bonds.  Higher convexity means:

- Price rises **more** than duration predicts when rates fall
- Price falls **less** than duration predicts when rates rise

This asymmetry is valuable — all else equal, investors prefer higher
convexity.

**Second-order price approximation:**

$$
\frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -D_{\text{mod}} \cdot \Delta y
  + \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot C \cdot (\Delta y)^2
$$

**Mini example — 5-year, 4 % semi-annual bond ($100 par, $y = 4\%$,
$D_{\text{mod}} = 4.49$, $C = 23.50$):**

| | Duration only | Duration + Convexity | Exact repricing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rates $+1\%$ | $-4.49\%$ | $-4.49\% + 0.12\% = -4.37\%$ | $-4.38\%$ |
| Rates $-1\%$ | $+4.49\%$ | $+4.49\% + 0.12\% = +4.61\%$ | $+4.61\%$ |

The convexity term ($\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 23.50 \cdot 0.01^2 = +0.12\%$)
always helps — it dampens losses and amplifies gains.

*Generated by `scripts/fixed_income_examples.py`.*

**Code:** `Bond.convexity()` in `src/alm/asset.py`

## DV01 (Dollar Value of a Basis Point)

**What it measures:**  The dollar change in a position's value for a
one-basis-point (0.01 %) parallel shift in rates.

**Formula (central finite difference):**

$$
\text{DV01} = \frac{PV(y - 0.0001) - PV(y + 0.0001)}{2}
$$

**Interpretation:**  If DV01 = \$44,913 on a \$100M bond, a 1 bp rate
increase reduces value by roughly \$44,913.

**Mini example — \$100M 5-year, 4 % semi-annual par bond:**

| | PV |
|---|---|
| Rate = 3.99 % | \$100,044,925 |
| Rate = 4.01 % | \$99,955,099 |
| **DV01** | **\$44,913** |

*Generated by `scripts/fixed_income_examples.py`.*

DV01 is the primary metric for measuring and hedging interest-rate
risk at the portfolio level, and the only rate-risk measure the toolkit
uses for net cash flows and swaps (their PV can be near zero, so
Macaulay duration is undefined for them).  Matching asset and liability
DV01s is the first-order condition of the hedge below.

**Code:** `dv01()` in `src/alm/core.py`

## Key Rate Duration (KRD)

!!! note "Concept only — not implemented in the toolkit"
    The toolkit discounts every instrument at a single flat rate and
    shifts that rate in parallel.  There is no yield curve to bump
    tenor by tenor, so no function computes KRDs and no KRD figures
    appear in the reports.  The section is here so the limitation of a
    parallel-shift hedge is named.

**What it measures:**  The sensitivity of a bond's price to a shift at
a single point on the yield curve, holding all other rates fixed.
KRDs decompose overall duration into contributions from specific
maturities.

**Formula:**

$$
\text{KRD}_k = -\frac{1}{P} \cdot \frac{\Delta P}{\Delta y_k}
$$

where $\Delta y_k$ is a bump at tenor $k$ only (typically 1 bp),
interpolated to nearby cash-flow dates.

**Key properties:**

- KRDs across all tenors sum to the bond's effective duration
- A bullet bond has KRD concentrated at its maturity
- A mortgage or amortizing bond has KRD spread across many tenors

**Why it matters:**  KRD analysis reveals mismatches that parallel
duration hedging misses.  A portfolio can be DV01-matched overall but
still lose surplus when the curve twists (short end rallies, long end
sells off).  The two-swap hedge below does nothing about that.

## Immunization

**What it means:**  Redington immunization protects a portfolio's
surplus (assets minus liabilities) against small parallel shifts in
interest rates.  Its three conditions are equal present values, equal
duration, and asset convexity at or above liability convexity.

The toolkit's `immunize()` implements the related two-instrument
surplus hedge: it matches DV01 (dollar duration) and dollar convexity
with two swaps, so surplus is flat to second order for a parallel
shift.  It does not enforce the present-value condition, so call the
result "DV01 and convexity matched" rather than "immunized."

### Duration Matching (First Order)

Match the dollar duration of assets to liabilities:

$$
\text{DV01}_{\text{assets}} = \text{DV01}_{\text{liabilities}}
$$

This neutralizes the portfolio against small parallel rate shifts.

### Convexity Matching (Second Order)

Duration matching alone leaves the portfolio exposed to larger rate
moves.  Adding a convexity match removes the second-order error for
parallel shifts (and nothing else: curve twists and spread moves are
not hedged):

$$
C_{\$,\text{assets}} = C_{\$,\text{liabilities}}
$$

where $C_\$$ is dollar convexity, $\partial^2 PV / \partial y^2$ in
dollars per unit of yield squared (`dollar_convexity()`).

### Two-Instrument DV01 and Convexity Matching

With two hedging instruments (e.g. a 5-year and 10-year swap), solve
the 2$\times$2 system:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
\text{DV01}_1 & \text{DV01}_2 \\
C_{\$,1} & C_{\$,2}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
n_1 \\ n_2
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\Delta\text{DV01} \\
\Delta C_\$
\end{bmatrix}
$$

where $\Delta\text{DV01}$ and $\Delta C_\$$ are the gaps to close
(liabilities minus assets), and $n_1, n_2$ are the required notionals.
A positive notional is a receive-fixed swap (adds DV01); a negative one
is pay-fixed.

**Units matter.**  DV01 is dollars per 1 bp; dollar convexity is
dollars per unit of yield squared.  For a real swap the second is tens
of thousands of times the first, so the two rows of the system live on
very different scales.

**Mini example:**  Both swaps are receive-fixed at 4 %, semi-annual,
valued on a flat 4 % curve.  Sensitivities are per \$1 of notional,
from `swap.dv01([0.04] * n, 0.04)` and
`dollar_convexity(lambda r: swap.present_value([r] * n, r), 0.04)`.

| Instrument | DV01 per \$1 notional | Dollar convexity per \$1 notional |
|---|---|---|
| 5-year receive-fixed swap | $4.491 \times 10^{-4}$ | 23.50 |
| 10-year receive-fixed swap | $8.176 \times 10^{-4}$ | 78.90 |

The gap comes from a \$100M block whose liabilities are proxied by a
10-year 4 % bond and whose assets are a 7-year 4 % bond (both at par,
so the PV condition holds):

| | DV01 | Dollar convexity |
|---|---|---|
| Liabilities (\$100M 10-year 4 % bond) | \$81,757 | \$7,889,793,319 |
| Assets (\$100M 7-year 4 % bond) | \$60,531 | \$4,260,106,626 |
| **Gap to close** | **\$21,226** | **\$3,629,686,692** |

Solving with `immunize(21_226, 3.63e9, 4.491e-4, 23.50, 8.176e-4, 78.90)`
(the script passes the unrounded values):

| Swap | Notional | Position |
|---|---|---|
| 5-year | −\$79,690,480 | pay-fixed |
| 10-year | +\$69,739,756 | receive-fixed |

Check: $n_1 \cdot \text{DV01}_1 + n_2 \cdot \text{DV01}_2$ = \$21,226
and $n_1 \cdot C_{\$,1} + n_2 \cdot C_{\$,2}$ = \$3,629,686,692, the
two gaps.  Repricing everything at 5 % and 3 %:

| Parallel shift | Surplus change, unhedged | Surplus change, hedged |
|---|---|---|
| +100 bp | +\$1,949,125 | +\$484 |
| −100 bp | −\$2,312,629 | −\$547 |

The residual is the third-order term the two-swap solve does not touch.
The pay-fixed 5-year leg looks odd until you notice the assets are
already too short: the 10-year swap alone would over-hedge DV01 while
under-hedging convexity, so the 5-year swap is sold to take DV01 back
out.

*Generated by `scripts/fixed_income_examples.py`.*

**Code:** `immunize()` in `src/alm/core.py`

## IRR (Internal Rate of Return)

**What it measures:**  The discount rate that makes the net present
value of a series of cash flows equal to zero.

**Formula:**

$$
\sum_{t=0}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1 + r)^t} = 0
$$

Solve for $r$.  There is no closed-form solution — the ALM toolkit
uses Newton-Raphson iteration.

**Newton-Raphson step:**

$$
r_{k+1} = r_k - \frac{NPV(r_k)}{NPV'(r_k)}
$$

where $NPV'(r) = \sum_{t=0}^{n} \frac{-t \cdot CF_t}{(1+r)^{t+1}}$.

**Mini example — \$100 investment returning \$30/yr for 4 years:**

| Year | Cash Flow |
|------|-----------|
| 0 | $-100$ |
| 1 | $+30$ |
| 2 | $+30$ |
| 3 | $+30$ |
| 4 | $+30$ |

Setting $NPV = 0$: $-100 + 30 \cdot \frac{1-(1+r)^{-4}}{r} = 0$

Solving: $r = 7.71\%$

*Generated by `scripts/fixed_income_examples.py`.*

**Code:** `irr()` in `src/alm/core.py`
